07 Self-determination theory

Three things have to be true
or nobody keeps going.

Deci and Ryan's model says motivation needs autonomy, competence and relatedness. Miss one and a student stops — not because the maths is hard, but because the loop stopped being worth it. PIA is built around all three.

  1. 01

    Autonomy

    You pick the tutor. You ask for the hint, or you don't. Nothing starts a topic on your behalf and nothing decides you're finished.

    In PIA: the agent is your choice, and it stays your choice.

  2. 02

    Competence

    Problems land just past what you can already do. Far enough to be worth solving, close enough that you get there.

    In PIA: difficulty follows your streak, not the class timetable.

  3. 03

    Relatedness

    Someone on the other side is paying attention. Not scoring you — remembering you.

    In PIA: your agent knows what tripped you up last session, and says so.

08 Capabilities

What PIA can do

  • 01

    Explains it again, differently

    If the first route doesn't land there's a second and a third. Same answer, different way in.

  • 02

    Moves at your speed

    Difficulty tracks what you just did, so nobody waits and nobody drowns.

  • 03

    Never runs a clock

    No timers, no streak you can break, no leaderboard. Nothing on screen is counting down.

  • 04

    Keeps your working private

    Your teacher sees that you're progressing. They don't see every wrong turn you took.

  • 05

    Runs on the school laptops

    A browser and a login. Nothing to install and nothing to carry home.

  • 06

    Picks up where you stopped

    Close the tab mid-problem. It's still there, mid-problem, when you come back.
